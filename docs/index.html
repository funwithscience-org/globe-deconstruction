Globe Deconstruction — A Claim-by-Claim Review

Answering the three claims at shapedebate.com and the twelve science questions in the book, one at a time.

This is an in-progress review of Globe Deconstruction?, a prerelease book by Levi Miller, and of the argument presented at shapedebate.com, which Miller runs with Alex Kampf. The book describes its position as geocentric flat-earth and treats the two labels as one package — its three headline claims are printed as “Geocentric flat-Earth claim #1/2/3”, and p. 109 asks “does he not know that ‘flat Earth’ includes geocentrism?” What it does not do is defend the standard flat-earth apparatus: the world-encompassing ice wall is “clearly just lazy” (p. 40), replacing gravity with relative density is “plain wrong!” (p. 57), and it tells flat-earthers to stop arguing from religious texts (p. 197). It is often sharper about flat-earth argument than its critics are. It also declines, deliberately, to advance a positive model at all — “falsification is independent of replacement” — which is a harder position to answer than the one usually attributed to it. The site's stated posture is “no religions, no beliefs, no biases, no assumptions,” and its authors invite exactly this sort of scrutiny. We are taking them at their word.

The finding so far

The book asks twenty-two versions of one question: does any single classical proof of the globe survive on its own? Often the honest answer is — not as cleanly as the textbook told you. Where his observations are real we concede them and measure them, and several pages here do exactly that. But the project has the argument backwards. The globe is not a stack of independent proofs any one of which carries the weight; it is one model that has to fit every observation at once, with one sun, one atmosphere, one optics. The book takes each observation in its own chapter and finds a separate explanation for it — and some of those explanations cannot both be true. Light travels in straight lines in the chapter on reflections and does not in the chapter on shadows. That is the finding, and the items below are instances of it.

How this review works

Each page answers one specific claim, as its author actually stated it — not a summary, not a weaker version, and not the general genre it belongs to. Where the underlying observation is real, or the arithmetic is right, we say so first and prominently, because a review that cannot concede anything is not a review. Where we think the reasoning fails, we show the working rather than asserting it, and we use the author's own figures wherever he supplies them. Where we genuinely do not know, we say that too. Every page ends with tests that would settle the question — and they have to be tests that could go against us, or they are not worth proposing.

The book charges the globe side with demanding laboratory control it never supplies itself, and on that charge it is right; our page on it concedes the point before anything else, and names an instance of our own double standard found while writing it. We take the same standard in return: where this review has published something wrong, the correction goes up with attribution rather than being quietly swapped out.

Why answer this at all

Most published responses to this material argue with a caricature. They answer the loudest version of a claim rather than the version actually made, and they treat any concession as a loss — which is precisely why they persuade nobody who wasn't already persuaded. The book notices this and says so, repeatedly and fairly.

So the standard here is higher than usual. If a claim is right, it gets conceded in full. If a claim is wrong, the demonstration has to be checkable by the reader without trusting us — public data, stated arithmetic, named sources. Several of these pages concede substantial ground: the curvature arithmetic in the pond test is correct, the perspective-compression physics in the angular-resolution chapter is real, Foucault pendulums genuinely are difficult to run well, and the book is unusually candid when its own evidence is thin. None of that is a problem for the review. It is what makes the rest of it worth reading.

We are in correspondence with the author, who has been generous about receiving criticism. Corrections are welcome and will be published: if any specific claim on any of these pages is wrong, tell us which one and why, and the correction goes up with attribution.

The foundations — the argument, made once

Twenty-two items answered one at a time would inherit the book's own problem: the point drowns in volume, and each chapter gets to be judged without the others in the room. So the general argument is made here, once, and the pages below link to it instead of re-arguing it. Nothing in this section substitutes for answering a claim on its merits — that still happens page by page.

The shape of the argument

Knowledge here is a mesh, not a chain. Read one at a time, the objections often land — Eratosthenes really does assume parallel rays and a sphere before it measures either, and a single pair of shadow angles genuinely cannot separate a far Sun over a globe from a near one over a plane. But the Sun's distance never rested on Eratosthenes. Radar ranging of Venus, stellar aberration and trigonometric parallax are three unrelated instruments returning one number, and knocking over one pillar of fifty leaves forty-nine standing. That is what the ledger below is for: a chapter-by-chapter book can put each proof on trial alone, and this review declines to re-prosecute settled measurements one page at a time.

There is no model on the other side to check, and that is deliberate rather than evasive: “it is much more effective not to present an alternative model at all” (p. 28), and “falsification is independent of replacement” (p. 187). The principle is correct and we concede it. You can show a thing is wrong without having a replacement ready, and demanding one is a bad habit of whichever side is defending. What it forfeits is that a falsification has to be a falsification of something. The local sun in one chapter, the non-straight light in another and the flat celestial plane in a third are never required to hold at the same time, because no chapter is answerable to the next — which is what the table below is about.

Settled once, then cited

Two kinds of thing sit here, and the distinction matters. The first three are established outside this review, by instruments that do not depend on one another; no page here proves them, they are cited. The fourth is different: the book makes rocket thrust in vacuum a headline claim, so this review does derive it — at length, on its own page — and the entry exists so that the rest of the site does not do it again. Either way the rule is the same: settle it once, then reference it.

  1. The Sun is about 93 million miles away. Radar ranging of Venus, stellar aberration, and trigonometric parallax — three unrelated techniques, one number. Does not rest on Eratosthenes, before or since. Established outside this review.
  2. The Earth turns once per sidereal day. Ring-laser gyroscopes at widely different latitudes each reading Ω sin φ, and satellite orbital dynamics. Established outside this review — though our drifters page recovers the period independently from public NOAA data, which is a check rather than the source.
  3. Light through air bends by a measured amount, not an assumed one. Geodesy does not assume straight sightlines; it carries a refraction coefficient, conventionally k ≈ 0.13, and corrects for it. Honest limit: near the ground k is not a constant and has been measured from −4 to +16, which is precisely why long-baseline work uses evacuated pipes. Established outside this review.
  4. Rocket thrust exists in vacuum. Derived here, not borrowed — it is Claim #2 and Q1, so Rockets Don't Push Against Air argues it from the nozzle outwards and Thrust, Measured in Flight gives the in-flight measurement record, cross-checked by mutually independent methods on the same engine with the ground-chamber artefact quantified at about one per cent, in the direction that flatters the chamber. Every other page cites those two rather than re-arguing thrust.

How claims are graded

Six verdicts, used unchanged across this project and its siblings Flat Earth Origins and the dome-model review, so a grade means the same thing everywhere. The verdict grades his claim; the smaller chip beside it tracks our progress. A verdict marked prov. rests on a page that has had one pass and no review. Items with no verdict have not been assessed — that is a real state, not an oversight.

Refuted
A specific measurement, dataset or source contradicts the claim.
Standard physics
The observation is real and the mainstream model already predicts it. It does not discriminate between the two models — in either direction.
Misleading
The conclusion outruns the evidence or the arithmetic that is supposed to carry it, or a cited source says something different from what is reported of it. This describes the argument, not the author.
Not demonstrated
Asserted without the evidence that would be needed to establish it.
Unfalsifiable
No observation could refute it as stated. Named, not ridiculed.
Self-contradicted
Collides with a position the same author takes elsewhere — the table above.

Standing rule: grade the claim as he actually hedged it, never a compressed or strengthened version. Where a page answers a stronger claim than the book makes, the item stays unassessed until that is fixed.

The three claims from shapedebate.com

These are the site's own headline claims, quoted as published.

  • Claim #1 · shapedebate.com

    “Objects can disappear bottom up across a flat surface due to the limitations of angular resolution.”

    MisleadingAnsweredBottom Up Observations, Answered — and The Black Swan, Answered, which answers the long-distance wind-farm footage the book cites as real-world evidence for it. In draft — published for review, not yet answers

These are unreviewed first drafts. They are published so they can be read in place and marked up, not because they are finished. None is linked as an answer in the catalogue above, each is marked noindex, and each carries a notice naming what is still outstanding on it.

The Full Moon Is Flat Because Dust Backscatters

Q7 · p. 50. His observation is right and so is the expectation behind it: a smooth diffusely reflecting sphere lit from behind the observer would be conspicuously darker at the limb, and the Moon is not. What that falsifies is Lambert's cosine law, which has been known to be the wrong law for dark particulate surfaces since the nineteenth century. Concedes that at full phase a Lommel-Seeliger sphere and a flat disc are photometrically identical, so the observation carries no information about shape either way.

The Sun Does Not Shrink

Q8 · p. 51. A receding Sun would shrink continuously and by a large factor across an afternoon. The measured angular diameter is flat to a fraction of a per cent. The fading is real and has a real cause — extinction along a long slant path — and the disc is occluded bottom-first by a sharp edge, which recession cannot produce.

Sunlight Bends in Air, and the Bend Is About One Arcminute

Q12 · p. 55. Refraction is real, and the page concedes that a single pair of shadow-angle stations genuinely cannot separate a curved Earth with a distant Sun from a flat Earth with a near one. The discrimination comes from measured degree lengths at different latitudes, which is a different measurement entirely.

Curvature Is in the Construction Record

Section p. 241. Concedes the premise outright: engineering documents mostly do not say "Earth's curvature". The argument is about procedures that would visibly fail if the term were absent — levelling that defines level as perpendicular to local gravity, radio path planning, tunnel geodesy — rather than about documents asserting anything.

The Standard of Proof Cuts Both Ways

Sections p. 203 + p. 415. The most self-critical page on the site. If the argument is that the two models face asymmetric standards of proof, that critique lands on this review too, and the page says so before it says anything else. The Antarctic magnetic half has been withdrawn — it answered a claim the book does not make; his actual argument is that declination itself is an invention.

The Sky Turns at One Rate, and Jupiter’s Shadows Point at One Sun

Q5 + Q6 · pp. 48–49. Q5's observation is right and understated — for stars the diurnal rate carries no distance information at one part in ten billion — because Earth's rotation changes where you point, not where you are. The distance signal lives in drift against the stars, a 2,203:1 spread that recovers the planets' orbits through Kepler's third law. Q6's shadow offsets are one angle applied to four orbits; they reverse sign across opposition, and an amateur imaged the January 2026 Callisto event. Closes with a signed prediction for November 2026 that has not happened yet.

Bending the Laws of Hydrostatics

Q2 · p. 45. His question is a fair one and the page concedes the core of it: nobody has run the controlled kilometre-long liquid-surface experiment he asks for, and in a uniform downward field water really would sit flat. The arithmetic gets corrected — the drop over 1,000 m is 19.6 mm, not three inches, because the chord and the tangent differ by a factor of four — and then the page explains why nobody builds the warehouse. A single hectopascal of air pressure across the hall moves the surface 10.19 mm, half the signal being looked for, which is why hydrostatic levelling systems use half-filled pipes and short spans. Water is a mediocre instrument at a kilometre; the straight machines that replaced it already show the answer.

Mirrored Reflections, Answered

Q4 · p. 47. A shore-level reflection is formed on 125 metres of water lying 41 to 166 metres from the photographer, over which the Earth departs from flat by three tenths of a millimetre. So the observation cannot discriminate between the models in either direction — which disposes of the claim and equally of any globe-side reply that treats a mirror-flat lake as evidence of curvature. The height-dependent test he proposes is real, and needs a temperature profile alongside it.

Supporting pages

One Spin, Written Across Every Latitude

Fifteen NOAA ocean drifters recover the length of the sidereal day to within half a per cent, and their loops reverse direction at the equator. Three models of Earth predict three different pictures; the data picks one. Supporting evidence for Q10 and Q11.

Self-Test Protocol

Seven falsifiable tests built from the book's own stated methodology — falsification-first, Team A versus Team B — that the author can run himself. Hand-off ready, no commentary.

Thrust, Measured in Flight

Ground vacuum chambers can always be accused of not being empty enough. Engines have also been measured firing in real space — and measured several physically unrelated ways at once. A 1993 NASA cross-check of four such methods on the same engine agrees to a few per cent, and quantifies the chamber artefact at about one per cent, in the direction that flatters the chamber.

The Action Lab Footage, Frame by Frame

The book argues at p. 20 that a third party's vacuum-chamber video accidentally disproves rocket thrust, because the syringe only moves once its exhaust reaches the wall. Measured frame by frame, the ordering is real and conceded — gas at the wall about two seconds of playback before first motion — an ordering neither account forbids, since a push too small to see looks identical to one not yet arrived. What the footage rules out is the wall as the cause: the delay is 25 to 220 times too slow for a pressure wave, returning smoke has no feature to copy, and thrust answers its own flare at the nozzle within a frame or two.

Something Did Disappear Over a Flat Surface

In August 2026 he towed a four-inch raft 540 feet across a pond and filmed it vanishing from a lens a third of an inch above the water. The curvature arithmetic on his slide is correct to the digit — a globe hides nothing at that range — so something local, roughly fifty times stronger than curvature could be, did the work. His own source files say what: from that vantage the raft and the entire pond behind it fit inside a single blur width, on a camera watching the scene with the equivalent of 20/100 vision. Refraction, the explanation most people reach for, is the one candidate the footage measurably excludes. Ends with the afternoon of tests that would settle it.

The Black Swan, Answered

A point-by-point response to the Rampion Wind Farm curvature video the book cites at pp. 91–95 as real-world evidence, including the two-century record of superior mirage on that stretch of English coast.

Gyroscopes and Earth's Rotation

A separate review, from an adjacent project: whether ring-laser and fibre-optic gyroscopes detect Earth's rotation, what instrument drift does and doesn't explain, and the 180° test that separates the two.

Status

Of the twenty-two items, sixteen carry a verdict and six do not — seven refuted, five misleading, four standard physics. Six of the sixteen are marked provisional, because the page behind them has had one pass and no review. The unassessed six are unassessed for stated reasons: two are catalogue entries we have found to be mis-sourced and have not yet rewritten, one is a cumulative claim that cannot be graded from a subset, one is a charge against this review that we accept rather than answer, and two have no page at all. This is an active review of a book that is itself still in prerelease, so both sides are moving. Pages are dated and revised in place rather than replaced, and where a page has changed materially in response to something the author said, it says so.

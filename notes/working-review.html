The two claims under active review

base claim recycled apparatus original

1. Rockets can't work in true vacuum

pp.16, 20, 26, 44, 77–84, 183, 196, 209–210, 232, 236–237, 258, 265

Gas-propelled thrust supposedly needs an external medium to push against — Miller states outright that "almost all flat earthers claim" this (a recycled premise). His original contribution is a self-funded ($3,000) vacuum-chamber syringe/toy-car experiment with Alex Kampf, formalized as "Kampf's Law" and called "the ultimate smoking gun of this entire book."

⚠ Correction made 2026-08-09

The only date anywhere in the 500-page text — "November 17, 2023" (p.20) — is a caption on a still from the Action Labs YouTube video that inspired their build, not the date of Miller/Kampf's own test (Steve confirmed this directly). Net effect: the book gives no date at all for their own experiment. Checked shapedebate.com directly on 2026-08-09: the /chamber page (linked from p.81, right at "Kampf's Law") is a stub — "This specific part of the book is not ready yet" — and the homepage lists the claim with status "2026 – Experiment done by Alex Kampf – waiting for upload." So this is an undated result, with a named law attached, presented as the book's decisive evidence, with zero public data behind it as of today.

Other side of it

Even once/if published: a $3,000 setup very likely means a rough mechanical-pump vacuum, not high vacuum. Residual gas pressure and wall-proximity effects in a small chamber are a plausible confound for "distance-dependent thrust delay" and would need to be ruled out (achieved pressure in torr/mbar, chamber dimensions, repeat trials) before the result says anything about true-vacuum propulsion.

original

2. Gravity's control over gases is unproven

pp.83–84, 102–104, 110, 211, 218–219

Never a single crisp thesis. Concentrated at pp.102–104 (how did volcanic gas ever escape Earth's gravity to make room for breathable air, if gravity holds gas down as a "blanket"?) and pp.211/218–219 (atmospheric confinement framed as a set of unproven "positive claims"). Reads as genuinely original — no match anywhere in the flat-earth-origins cluster catalogue. Notably distinct from — and explicitly rejected in favor of, over — the standard flat-earth "buoyancy/density replaces gravity" line, which Miller calls out by name as wrong (p.1, p.197).

largely recycled, already-explained

3. "The Sun is not the illumination source"

pp.26, 75–78, 160–161, 170–173, 186–192, 194–195, 441, 5527–5533 — shapedebate.com/moontilt, shapedebate.com/tfe

Formal statement (p.26/441/5527): "There are too many red flags in celestial observations. The four hardest to ignore are the uniform light distribution of a full moon, the moon-tilt illusion, Jupiter's moon shadows that fail to align heliocentrically, and the failed trajectory alignment during a solar eclipse. Together, they make a strong case that the Sun is not the illumination source."

Broader context: pp.191–192 list a longer Sun/Moon/Star "red flag" bucket (cloud hotspots, temperature gradients, sun dogs, selenelion eclipses, star parallax skepticism), plus a separate "local light source" strand (pp.170–173) arguing sunset lighting variance fits a nearby, contracting/expanding local heat source better than a fixed 93-million-mile Sun. The claim explicitly ties itself to Antarctica/TFE: "If the Antarctica Final Experiment is confirmed faked by independent research, the game will be over." Unlike the two signature claims, three of the four named sub-claims land on real, previously-studied astronomical phenomena with accepted explanations — this plank reads as misapplied/incomplete physics more than undiscovered anomaly. The fourth (eclipse trajectory) can't yet be assessed from the extracted text alone.

Framing note

This is not a standard flat-earth text. Miller self-identifies as "geocentrist," not "flat earther," and repeatedly, explicitly disowns several mainline flat-earth positions: the Antarctic ice-wall/unexplored-continent narrative ("clearly just lazy," pp.40, 66), the Gleason map ("just a distorted version of the globe," p.146), and — most relevant here — the buoyancy/density-replaces-gravity explanation itself ("this is clearly not the case," p.1; "stop using relative density as a replacement for... gravity," p.197). Treating this as generic flat-earth material will misfire in places.

Other major planks (full detail in the organized breakdown)

Bottom-up disappearance / angular resolution UNCLEAR / leans recycled

Argues ships/cities disappear "bottom-up" over water due to limits of human/camera angular resolution + linear perspective, not curvature. Illustrated with headlight/hallway/tree-line analogies and long-zoom photography (Nikon P900/P1000 shots of a wind farm and Monviso, Italy at 184 miles).

Water / hydrostatics recycled base claim, some original test designs

No controlled experiment has ever directly measured water bending to Earth's curvature (matches Rowbotham's classic "water finds its level" argument) — proposes an original hypothetical 1000-meter "skinny warehouse" test to settle it. Also: Lake Pontchartrain refraction dispute, Lake Manly/Lake Pukaki reflection-geometry proposal, theodolite/refraction-constant critique of "end of flat earth" shadow experiments.

Celestial / optics claims mixed

Argues the eye's spherical geometry — not sky curvature — explains why Polaris appears to rise/fall with latitude; calculates Polaris's angular size as ~18,860× smaller than the eye's resolution limit; critiques a flat-earth-challenge astrophotographer's Jupiter moon-shadow image as misaligned with the Sun.

Astronautics / ISS / NASA skepticism largely recycled

Standard "ISS is CGI/wire-work/green-screen" catalogue and Massimo Mazzucco-sourced moon-landing-hoax claims. Separately: an extended, detailed treatment of Bob Knodel's 2018 ring-laser gyroscope experiment from Behind the Curve — argues suppressed elevation-dependent follow-up data actually disproves Earth's rotation. This is a near-exact match to an existing, well-documented flat-earth argument (Knodel/GlobeBusters, 2018).

Atmospheric co-rotation skepticism recycled, unattributed

Standard "why doesn't air's rotational speed vary by altitude, and why is air speed zero exactly at the pole" argument — a long-standing Rowbotham-lineage claim, presented without attribution.

Antarctica / cartography original speculation, explicitly self-labeled

Rejects the standard ice-wall narrative outright. Instead builds an original, self-labeled-speculative reinterpretation of the 1587 Urbano Monte world map — arguing its "errors" may reflect real lost geography, that Antarctica's "land of fire" label implies it was once ice-free, and sketching a new "Magnetic Declination Removed" flat map with hypothetical unexplored lands ("Lacach," "Patalia," "Galleca," "Vista").

Historical / architecture "Tartaria" tangents recycled genre, ~pp.241–470, self-flagged as off-topic

Roughly a third of the book (pp.241–470): elaborate 19th-century architecture supposedly inconsistent with claimed construction dates/technology, "mudflood" buried first floors, missing construction records — the well-known "Tartaria" internet-conspiracy genre, explicitly credited to other YouTube researchers rather than original to Miller. Culminates in an original "Reset War" theory tying 1850–1880 conflicts to a coordinated global rollout of globe-based school curricula. Miller himself flags this whole section as outside the core "shape debate."

Rhetorical structure

Opens with an extended epistemology/cognitive-bias primer (indoctrination, confirmation bias, normalcy bias, dogmatism) before any substantive claims. Repeatedly frames the whole project as structurally identical to atheist "deconstruction" of religion — Miller narrates his own break from Christianity as a template, dedicates the book to Alex Hormozi and Matt Dillahunty, and brands it "No religions. No beliefs. No biases. No assumptions." Central dialectical device: a "falsification-only" burden-of-proof scorecard (pp.205–230) cataloguing 100+ ordinary astronomy facts as unverified "positive claims" heliocentrism must defend.
